Ishmael describes taking his first turn at the mast-head lookout. He surveys the history of elevated watching posts — Egyptians atop the Pyramids, Napoleon on his column, Washington on his monument. He warns against the danger of sending a dreamy, philosophically-inclined young man aloft: such a person, lulled by the sea's motion and his own reverie, will lose all sense of self, merge with the infinite, and forget entirely to watch for whales — then tumble from the mast into the sea below.
