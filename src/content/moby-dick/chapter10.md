Back at the Spouter-Inn, Ishmael watches Queequeg solemnly counting the pages of a large book in groups of fifty, astonished by the total. Drawn to Queequeg's quiet dignity and self-possession, Ishmael approaches. They share a pipe, bond immediately, and Queequeg declares them "married" — bosom friends in his culture's phrase. He divides his silver coins equally and gives Ishmael half. That evening they join together in Queequeg's idol-worship, Ishmael reasoning that to worship with a friend is simply to do the will of God.
